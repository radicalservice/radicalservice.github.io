Why component-level repair matters

Most Apple devices that "die" are not beyond repair. Very often the fault is a single failed part on the logic board: a shorted capacitor, a damaged power management IC or a corroded connector after liquid exposure. Swapping the whole board fixes the symptom, but it is expensive and usually wipes the user's data.

Component-level repair is done under a stereo microscope with micro-soldering and BGA rework tools. It replaces only the part that actually failed, which saves the board, the data and a lot of electronic waste.

Data recovery on modern MacBooks

On MacBooks with the T2 chip or Apple Silicon, storage is soldered to the board and encrypted by it. Chip-off recovery and recovery software cannot read it. The only way to get the data back is to repair the logic board until the machine boots again, which is exactly what board-level repair does.

Independent or authorised?

If a device is still under Apple's warranty, an Apple Authorised Service Provider is the right choice, because the repair costs you nothing. Outside warranty, a specialised independent workshop is usually faster, often cheaper, and able to repair boards that would otherwise simply be replaced.
